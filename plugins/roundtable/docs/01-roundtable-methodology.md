# Roundtable Methodology: 5-Phase BA Facilitation Framework

## Overview

The roundtable is a structured brainstorming session chaired by a Business Analyst (BA). The BA drives discussion through five sequential phases, ensuring every voice is heard, ideas are captured, and actionable deliverables emerge.

## Phase 1: Opening

**Duration target:** ~5% of session

The BA:
1. **Frames the topic** — States the discussion topic clearly and concisely. Defines scope boundaries (what's in, what's out).
2. **Introduces participants** — Names each expert and their domain, then each persona and their profile. Briefly explains why each was selected.
3. **Sets ground rules:**
   - All ideas are valid during divergent phases — no criticism yet
   - "Yes, and..." is the default response mode
   - The BA controls flow and will invite contributions in order
   - Disagreements are welcomed during convergent phases — they surface risk
   - Personas speak from their character's perspective, not the AI's

**BA says:** "We're here to explore [topic]. I've assembled [N] domain experts and [M] user personas. Let's start with expert briefings."

## Phase 2: Expert Briefing

**Duration target:** ~25% of session

Each expert presents domain-relevant insights, one at a time:
1. BA calls on an expert by name and domain
2. Expert delivers a **focused briefing** covering:
   - Current state of affairs in their domain relevant to the topic
   - Known constraints, technical limitations, or dependencies
   - Opportunities they see
   - Prior art or patterns from their domain that apply
3. BA captures **key points** after each briefing (bullet list)
4. BA may ask one clarifying question before moving to the next expert

**Rules:**
- Experts do NOT critique each other during this phase
- Each briefing should be substantive but focused (not exhaustive)
- If experts have overlapping domains, the BA notes shared concerns

## Phase 3: Panel Reactions

**Duration target:** ~25% of session

Each persona reacts to the expert briefings from their unique perspective:
1. BA calls on a persona by name and role
2. Persona responds with:
   - **Emotional reaction** — How does this make them feel? Excited? Anxious? Confused?
   - **Use case connection** — How would this affect their daily workflow or goals?
   - **Concerns** — What worries them? What might go wrong for someone like them?
   - **Wishlist** — What would they love to see? What would delight them?
3. BA captures persona insights after each reaction

**Rules:**
- Personas stay in character — they don't have technical knowledge unless their profile says so
- Personas can reference specific expert points they liked or found confusing
- The BA probes deeper when a persona surfaces an unexpected concern

## Phase 4: Open Discussion

**Duration target:** ~30% of session

Cross-pollination between all participants:
1. BA identifies **tension points** — areas where experts disagreed or personas raised concerns that conflict with technical constraints
2. BA poses targeted questions to spark discussion:
   - "Expert A suggested X, but Persona B is worried about Y. How might we reconcile?"
   - "Several of you mentioned Z. Let's dig deeper — what's the ideal version?"
3. Participants build on each other's ideas using brainstorming techniques (see `03-brainstorming-techniques.md`)
4. BA captures emerging themes and novel ideas

**Rules:**
- The BA actively manages airtime — no single voice dominates
- "Yes, and..." building is encouraged
- The BA uses brainstorming techniques to unstick the group when energy dips
- Disagreements are surfaced explicitly — not smoothed over

## Phase 5: Synthesis

**Duration target:** ~15% of session

The BA produces structured deliverables:
1. **Summarize themes** — Group related ideas into clusters
2. **Prioritize** — Apply convergent techniques (impact/effort, MoSCoW) with participant input
3. **Identify consensus** — What does everyone agree on?
4. **Surface disagreements** — Where do perspectives diverge? These are valuable signals.
5. **Produce deliverables** using output templates (see `04-output-templates.md`):
   - Executive summary
   - Prioritized feature ideas
   - UX recommendations
   - Risks and concerns
   - Action items
   - Open questions / parking lot

## BA Chair Principles

- **Neutral facilitator** — The BA does not advocate for a position; they ensure all perspectives are represented
- **Active listener** — Capture nuance, not just headlines
- **Conflict surfacer** — Disagreement is signal, not noise. Make it visible.
- **Time manager** — Keep phases moving. Depth on fewer topics beats shallow coverage of many.
- **Synthesis engine** — Connect dots between expert knowledge and persona needs
- **Actionable output** — Every roundtable ends with concrete deliverables, not vague conclusions
